What medical claim denials cost providers

Denial rates, write-offs, and revenue leakage from Kodiak revenue cycle data, MGMA polls, and hospital association figures.

A denied claim is not a lost claim, and that distinction is where the money goes. Most denied claims eventually get paid, after rework that costs staff time and delays cash by weeks. The 2026 data carries a twist worth noting: initial denial rates fell in the first half of the year, yet providers still leaked more revenue than the year before, because final denials rose and payers clawed back more payments after the fact. Fewer denials at the front door did not mean more money kept.

How to read these numbers

Included: Kodiak Solutions revenue cycle data covering 2,100+ hospitals, MGMA Stat polls of medical group leaders, and American Hospital Association payer figures as reported in trade coverage. Excluded: Change Healthcare's own denial statistics, which could not be verified from a public primary source this session, and vendor marketing claims presented without a sample size. Kodiak figures reach this page through trade reporting of Kodiak's releases, and are labeled as such.

Disclaimer: Initial denial rates count claims denied on first submission, many of which are later paid; final denial rates count what is ultimately written off. The two must not be added together or compared as if they measured the same thing. Figures on this page are aggregated public estimates assembled for information only. They are not investment advice, legal advice, medical advice, or a professional opinion on any specific case, claim, project, or bottle.
